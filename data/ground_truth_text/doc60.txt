How Photosynthesis Works
Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae and some bacteria turn light energy into chemical energy. It takes place mainly in the leaves, inside small structures called chloroplasts that contain a green pigment named chlorophyll.
The process has two main stages. In the first stage, chlorophyll absorbs sunlight and uses that energy to split water molecules into hydrogen and oxygen. The oxygen is released into the air, which is the gas that most living things breathe. The energy captured in this stage is stored in small molecules that act like rechargeable batteries.
In the second stage, the plant uses the stored energy to combine carbon dioxide from the air with hydrogen to build sugar. This sugar feeds the plant, and the extra is stored as starch in roots, seeds and fruits. Humans and animals depend on these stored foods.
Several factors affect how fast photosynthesis happens: the amount of light, the temperature, the supply of water and the concentration of carbon dioxide. Without enough water, for example, the leaf closes tiny openings called stomata to avoid drying out, and this also blocks the entry of carbon dioxide. That is why plants grow slowly during a long drought.
