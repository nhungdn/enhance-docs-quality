A Short History of the Bicycle
The first ancestor of the bicycle was built in 1817 by a German baron named Karl von Drais. His machine, called the draisine, had two wheels in a line and a wooden frame, but no pedals. The rider sat on it and pushed against the ground with both feet, much like a child on a modern balance bike.
In the 1860s, French inventors added pedals directly to the front wheel. This design, known as the velocipede, was heavy and uncomfortable, and its iron tyres earned it the nickname boneshaker. Later models made the front wheel very large so that each turn of the pedals would carry the rider farther, but these high wheelers were difficult to mount and dangerous in a fall.
The big change came in the 1880s with the safety bicycle. It had two wheels of equal size and a chain that drove the rear wheel, which made it stable and easy to ride. In 1888, John Boyd Dunlop developed the pneumatic tyre, filling it with air to absorb bumps and make the ride smoother and faster.
Today, the bicycle remains one of the most efficient machines ever invented. It is cheap, needs no fuel and keeps its rider healthy. Many cities are building bike lanes to reduce traffic and pollution, and electric bicycles now help people travel longer distances with less effort.
